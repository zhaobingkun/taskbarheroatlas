## TBH Beginner Route: Find Your First Wall Before Chasing a Build

**Checked against the official v1.01.04 hotfix on August 2, 2026.**

There is no useful permanent “best build” if it does not solve the problem stopping your current run. This guide is a first-hours decision route: find the wall, make one focused change, then test it in the same activity.

I made this independently as a player resource. It is not affiliated with the developers, and I will update or correct it when a source or repeatable test says it should change.

### 1. Start by naming the actual problem

Before changing a class, Rune, or item, put your current issue in one of these three groups:

**Repeated clears are slow**

You need more consistent clear speed. Test this by repeating the same activity for a fixed amount of time and noting how far you get or how many runs finish.

**One hard target stops the run**

You need a better hard-target plan. Keep the stage and difficulty the same while you test; otherwise you will not know whether the change helped.

**The run ends before damage matters**

You need survival or stability. A damage-only change may look good on paper while producing fewer completed runs.

Do not call a build bad until you know which of these problems it was meant to solve.

### 2. Make one change, not five

The fastest way to learn nothing is to replace your whole setup at once. Use this simple loop instead:

1. Write down the patch, activity, stage/difficulty, and the problem above.
2. Keep the same activity for the test.
3. Change one meaningful part of the setup: a Rune goal, gear priority, class slot, or skill focus.
4. Repeat the activity enough times to rule out a single lucky or unlucky run.
5. Keep the change only if it improves the result that was actually blocking you.

This is slower than copying a screenshot, but it produces a build you can explain and update after a patch.

### 3. Treat role access and role strength as separate questions

Some roles are available through DLC. That does not make any role automatically required, and it does not prove a paid role is stronger in every activity.

When deciding whether to use or buy a role, ask:

- What problem would this role solve in my current run?
- Can my existing setup solve that problem with a smaller change first?
- Have I tested it in the same stage and difficulty, rather than comparing different runs?

Avoid buying a role just because someone labels it “S tier” without a patch, activity, setup, and result.

### 4. Spend Rune points for the next wall

Rune choices should have a job. A route for repeated clears may not be the route for a difficult target, and neither may help if your party cannot stay alive.

Before spending a point, write a sentence such as:

> “I am changing this because repeated clears are slow in this activity.”

or:

> “I am changing this because the run ends before I can finish the hard target.”

If you cannot write that sentence, save the point until you can. It is better to have a reasoned route than a route copied from a different patch or a different goal.

### 5. Keep a tiny test note

Use this format in a note, spreadsheet, or Steam comment:

`Patch | Activity | Stage/Difficulty | Problem | One change | Result | Keep / revert`

Example:

`v1.01.04 | [activity] | [stage] | repeated clears slow | [one change] | [result] | keep/revert`

The result does not need to be a perfect damage calculation. A clear observation such as “more completed runs in the same time” or “still dies before the target” is enough to decide the next test.

### 6. Do not turn unknown data into advice

Chest schedules, drop rates, market values, and balance effects can change or may differ by activity. Do not assume a community claim is official just because it is repeated.

For game facts, check the official Steam announcements first. For player testing, keep the patch and setup attached to the result so someone else can reproduce or challenge it.

### A quick first-hours checklist

- Pick one activity, not every activity at once.
- Identify clear speed, hard-target damage, or survival as the immediate wall.
- Change one thing.
- Repeat the same test.
- Record the result and keep only what helped.
- Re-check after a patch instead of assuming yesterday's answer is permanent.
